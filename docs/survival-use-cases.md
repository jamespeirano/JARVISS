# Survival use cases

## Practical scenarios

1. **“Is this water usable?”** You find a well, rain barrel, or stream. It helps identify contamination risks and consult downloaded treatment guidance.
2. **“Can I still eat this?”** Your refrigerator lost power. It helps assess food using elapsed time and temperature, rather than relying on smell.
3. **“What does this warning mean?”** A generator, battery, or inverter stops working. It finds the relevant manual and explains the fault.
4. **“Will these parts work together?”** You find solar panels, batteries, cables, and chargers. It helps interpret their specifications and identify incompatible combinations.
5. **“How do we manage toilets without running water?”** It retrieves sanitation guidance and helps plan around your household, available space, and supplies.
6. **“Can we stay in this building?”** It walks through documented warning signs—gas leaks, fire damage, flooding—and identifies situations requiring evacuation or expert inspection.
7. **“What does this label say?”** It translates instructions on food, tools, equipment, or medical supplies without internet.
8. **“How do I fix this with what I have?”** A bicycle, hand pump, or water filter breaks. It searches repair instructions and identifies the tools and replacement parts needed.
9. **“What can I grow here, and when?”** It uses downloaded regional planting guides, your seed inventory, and local observations.
10. **“How do we find each other?”** It helps operate available radios using their manuals and establish meeting points and communication schedules.
11. **“Which task matters first?”** You have limited daylight, water, and battery power. It turns those constraints into an actionable plan.

## Additional uses

1. **Supply tracking:** Estimate how long food, water, fuel, batteries, and medication will last.
2. **Daily planning:** Prioritize tasks based on immediate needs, available daylight, weather observations, and energy.
3. **Offline navigation:** Find mapped water sources, shelters, clinics, and routes—with clear warnings that conditions may have changed.
4. **Power budgeting:** Plan device use, charging, and solar generation to keep essential tools running longer.
5. **Local knowledge library:** Search downloaded repair manuals, agricultural guides, sanitation instructions, and first-aid references.
6. **Equipment repair:** Walk through troubleshooting using manuals for the equipment you actually own.
7. **Food production:** Maintain planting calendars, seed inventories, crop records, and preservation references.
8. **Community coordination:** Track people’s skills, shared resources, responsibilities, and requests for help.
9. **Local communication:** Exchange messages and updates over a working local network without internet or satellites.
10. **Situation logging:** Record hazards, observations, routes, and decisions, keeping confirmed facts separate from assumptions.
11. **Education:** Provide offline lessons, practical training, translation, and accessible voice instructions.
12. **Rebuilding:** Organize repair projects, materials, dependencies, and maintenance schedules.

## How could it be faster?

There are three separate meanings of speed:

1. **Development throughput:** coding agents can automate implementation, testing, and iteration, and independent work can run concurrently. This is a plausible mechanism for faster delivery, but does not establish the stipulated 100–1000× multiplier.
2. **AI improving AI:** better models assist research and engineering for subsequent models, potentially shortening successive improvement cycles. Amodei argues this is already accelerating progress; the magnitude is not independently measured here. See the [dated news record](news-2026-09-13.md).
3. **World adoption:** deployment, procurement, trust, infrastructure, and institutional responses determine how capability gains affect everyday life. Faster software production does not make these delays vanish.

Illustrative arithmetic, not a measurement: if coding takes 80% of a project's elapsed time and becomes 100× faster, while the remaining 20% is unchanged, total speedup is 1 / (0.2 + 0.8 / 100), or approximately 4.81×. A 1000× coding speedup yields approximately 4.98×. This assumes sequential work and fixed scope; it shows why a component multiplier cannot automatically be applied to the entire process.

The opening question, 'How is it faster?', did not identify a comparison in this task. These mechanisms explain the acceleration thesis; they do not claim a measured advantage over an unspecified alternative.

## What would change the assessment?

- Comparable end-to-end delivery measurements could replace the software-speed assumption.
- Measurements of AI's contribution to research cycles could strengthen or weaken the compounding-progress hypothesis.
- Actual adoption, workflow changes, and institutional actions over weeks and months could test the disruption timeline.
- Durable, verifiable coordination could weaken the expectation of an unchecked race.
- Independent incident investigations and safety evaluations could revise the risk assessment.

No probability of catastrophe, guaranteed superintelligence date, or inevitable collapse was established in the reviewed discussions.
